How a fundraising platform charges for its service says a lot about who it is for. Charge per donation and donors see their TZS 1,000 arrive as TZS 950 — progress that silently erodes trust. Charge by subscription and an organization's cost scales with nothing. Changia uses a third model: a configurable service fee added to the required campaign amount. Here is why that single decision keeps the entire model honest.

## The Problem With Per-Donation Fees

A fee deducted from each contribution is invisible to the donor's receipt but visible in the campaign's balance. When every donor's gift is reduced before it reaches the goal, the public progress bar stops being truthful, and the organization must explain why collected money never equals announced money.

Per-donation fees also complicate reconciliation: variable amounts, rounding and refunds all multiply. The accounting burden lands exactly where the organization is least equipped to handle it.

## The Campaign-Level Model

Changia's model is simple. When a campaign is created, a service fee — for example 5% — is calculated from the purpose amount and added to it. The public collection target therefore includes the platform fee from the start.

The result is honest progress. Every individual donation is credited at its full face value, so TZS 1,000 given equals TZS 1,000 shown. Donors see the total goal, the amount raised and the exact difference — no arithmetic surprises at the end.

## A Worked Example: The Children Surgery Fund

Consider a campaign with a purpose amount of TZS 10,000,000 and a 5% Changia fee. The service fee is TZS 500,000, and the public collection target becomes TZS 10,500,000. After the full target is reached, the owner allocation is TZS 10,000,000 and the Changia allocation is TZS 500,000.

Because the fee is disclosed and fixed up front, every stakeholder can verify the math. The campaign owner sees exactly what they will receive; donors see exactly what the appeal must raise.

## What Stays Out of the Fee

Transparency also means being clear about what the fee does not cover. Payment gateway charges, SMS and messaging bills, domain and hosting, and legal or tax advice are external costs handled with the relevant providers. On the campaign page and in the owner approval screens, ecosystem fees are disclosed so nobody receives a surprise at settlement.

## Transparency Obligations

A fee model only earns trust when it is disclosed. The public campaign page and the campaign-owner approval screen must state the purpose amount, the applicable service fee, the public collection target, applicable gateway or telecom charges, and the campaign closure and payout policy. When every number is visible, there is nothing left to argue about.

## Conclusion

A campaign-level fee is a small design decision with large consequences. It makes progress bars truthful, reconciliation simple and discussions about money rare. For organizations, that is the difference between a platform that collects funds and one that builds lasting trust.
